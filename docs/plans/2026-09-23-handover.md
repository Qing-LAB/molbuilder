## 2. Transport — row **W30**, and the one decision

### The design, in one sentence

**The surface is TWO surfaces**: one *shared panel* that edits the **template**
and binds all five rungs, and one *per-rung form* that edits a rung's
**override bag**. Both are generated from the catalogue, narrowed by kind, and
the markers decide which value lands where (§ 3.8.2).

Every failed attempt has produced either a form that hides the shared values
with nowhere to put them, or a form that offers them and is refused
downstream. That is the whole history of this surface.

### The gap that blocks everything — there is no marker for SHARED

`citation` says *who supplies the default*. Class A (§ 2a.13) is **larger**:
`species_order`, `spin_treatment`, `spin_total` and the pseudopotentials bind
every rung and **no run answers them**.

Treating the two as the same thing was measured on 2026-09-23 and cost a
revert. The catalogue swap filtered on `citation`, so the form offered
`system_label`, `species_order`, `spin_treatment` and `spin_total` as per-rung
overrides, all routed to the device:

* a `system_label` override **survives into three rungs' decks** — `prep`
  replaces it only when `label != task.label` — so SIESTA writes
  `<their string>.TS.HSX` while the transmission rung consumes
  `<task_label>.TS.HSX`. The handover fails. On the two electrode rungs the
  same control is silently **inert**;
* a `species_order` override gives the device one `ChemicalSpeciesLabel` order
  and the leads the default — the disagreement `model/chemistry.md` § 3a was
  written that morning to make impossible.

> **`prep` has the same hole and it predates the swap.** Its shared-value
> refusal also gates on `citation=True`, so a stage override of
> `species_order` has never been refused. The swap did not create the hole; it
> made it reachable from the UI. **One declaration fixes both doors.**

**The choice (§ 3.8.6), and it is the user's:** a sibling marker
`shared = ["transport"]` beside `citation`, or widen `citation` to mean
*shared, and here is who defaults it*.

### Order of work — W30's ① to ⑤, which replaces six competing orderings

An inventory read four documents in full and found **15 contradictions and six
separate orders of work**. W30 absorbs them; do not follow the older ones.

1. **the SHARED declaration** — then form, describe door and `prep` all read
   one declaration, which closes the prep hole and re-enables the reverted swap;
2. **the shared panel** — until it exists there is *nowhere in the UI* to state
   the electronic description, so a hand-built structure cannot be described;
3. **unanswered `citation` rows stay VALUELESS** at `init` — ② and ③ land
   together or the plain-structure road regresses between them;
4. **the deck viewer**, which **consumes** § 5c.3's calculation-root reader and
   does not write its own;
5. **the per-engine panel split** (W24 / § 5o.5 step 4), unchanged by this row.

### Two traps, both already sprung once

* **Do not re-attempt the catalogue swap before ①.** The filter it condemns is
  measured dead in seven of ten branches and the temptation is obvious; the
  reasoning is recorded at the filter itself in `blueprints/transport.py`.
* **Do not write a second directory enumerator for the deck viewer.** The
  warning is § 5c.3's and it was right — but **its proposed owner is stale**.
  Re-measured 2026-09-23: the reader that exists is **`calcdirs.container_or_run`**
  (a new module, 2026-09-19), not the `checkpoint._is_bundle_root` § 5c.3 names,
  which is still private with one caller. Consume `calcdirs`.
  *(And note the irony worth not repeating: § 5c.3 warned that a second
  predicate for "is this a calculation root" would be "instance 14" of a
  duplication problem, and one landed anyway — the two now answer on
  different evidence, `_is_bundle_root` on which files exist and
  `container_or_run` on `task.json`'s `shape`.)*

## 3. Spectrum — row **W21**, already consolidated

Unlike transport, this was designed in one place and has not drifted. **The
design is agreed; the two prerequisites are server-side and neither is built.**

**What is measured today:** the run computes both channels into
`<job>.spectra.json` — per mode, `raman_activity_a4_amu` *and*
`ir_intensity_km_mol` side by side — and the viewer shows only Raman. It
touches the IR field in exactly one place, inside a change-detection
fingerprint: it reads IR to notice the data changed and never to draw it. So
ticking *Compute IR intensities* produces correct physics a person can only
read by opening the JSON.

**The shape agreed with the user:** ① a mirror plot — Raman up on a left axis,
IR down on a right axis, each axis coloured to its curve, because both channels
peak at the same frequencies and one half-plane makes them collide; ② a rug of
ticks along y=0 carrying **every** mode, coloured by class (Raman-only ·
IR-only · both · silent), position only and never a height, because a silent
mode given a stick height is a lie in either direction; ③ a relative threshold
slider — *"above X% of the strongest peak in its own channel"* — one control
for two incommensurate units.

**The two prerequisites, and they are why this is not a front-end task:**

1. **`spectra.json` carries no activity classification**, and the zeros are
   numerical, not exact — a real CO₂ run's silent entries are `4.8e-09`,
   `1.0e-08`, `7.5e-08`. So *"is this IR-active"* is a decision needing ONE
   home, stored, never a magic epsilon invented in the viewer.
2. **The per-mode electronic-structure probe selects its modes by Raman
   brightness** (`vibration_emitters.py` ranks on `raman_activity_a4_amu` and
   cuts on a threshold whose config label is literally *"Raman-activity
   threshold"*). Gap modulation is ∂ε/∂Q; Raman is ∂α/∂Q — different selection
   rules, and **in a centrosymmetric molecule the filter is wrong in a
   systematic direction**, keeping the gerade modes and dropping every
   IR-active one.

Note the two thresholds are distinct and must stay so: `es_threshold` decides
what is **computed** (2 SCFs per mode) and stays absolute; the new one decides
what is **shown** and is free.

**Related open rows:** **V1** (vibrations with held atoms — one path, not two),
**E11** (a fresh live walk of the PySCF/spectra decks), **P11** (a two-case
test that tests one).

## 5. Two items that are NOT transport or spectrum, and need checking

### 5.1 `structure_hash` — already being worked elsewhere; please cross-check

**Another machine is working on this now.** What follows is what was measured
here on 2026-09-23, so that work can be checked against it rather than
duplicating the investigation.

Every `.xyz` + `.molstruct.json` pair carries `structure_hash` — the SHA-256
of the geometry document — written by `workingcopy_structure.py` on every
save. **Nothing verifies it.** `sidecars/molstruct.load`'s own comment says
*"`structure_hash` is NOT verified here (the caller compares it against the
…)"*, and `StructureCodec.load` — the caller — does not. Grepped the tree: no
comparison anywhere.

*What it is for, and it is not file integrity.* The pair is written
atomically, so molbuilder cannot leave the halves out of step. What can is a
person editing the `.xyz` in a text editor months later — entirely
legitimate, and the reason the field exists. The consequence is not a corrupt
file: the sidecar's labels are stored as **atom indices**, so if atoms were
added, removed or reordered those indices now name different atoms; and its
`info.calculation` block was measured on coordinates that have changed. A
form-B citation inherits that block (§ 3.1's middle case), so a stale hash is
how a mesh cutoff converged for one geometry is applied to another.

*The shape the user specified* (2026-09-23): detect on load; **do not**
auto-repair; **do not** refuse — *reading does not judge*, and a file you
cannot open is a file you cannot fix. Provide a separate door the person
calls **after checking the file**, which re-stamps the hash. That call IS the
confirmation, so it needs no prompt.

> **The naming matters and is the whole point.** Re-stamping is an
> **attestation, not a repair**: it says *"I have looked, and the labels still
> apply."* Only the person who made the edit can say that. A function named
> like a fix invites exactly the automatic use this design rules out.

### 5.2 When is a metadata strip RIGHT? — needs validation

**Status: a rule derived here from one worked case, written into a code
comment, and NOT into the document that owns the concept. It should be
validated or corrected before it is relied on.**

`model/structure.md` § 2.2a says a strip of `info` must be **explicit** and
calls a silent one a defect. It gives **no test for when a strip is
correct** — so the next person meets the question with nothing to read, which
is what happened here.

*The worked case.* `wizard.as_structure()` builds a lead from a junction and
carries no `info`. Asked whether that is a defect under § 2.2a, the answer
came out the other way: the junction's `info.calculation` records a mesh
cutoff and transverse k **converged for the junction's cell**, and the lead
has a different cell (its own bulk z-period) and a different k-grid (dense
along transport). A lead carrying that block would assert settings measured
for a geometry that is not its own — and `structure_modified` would not be
set, because extraction is not an edit. That is precisely the failure the
flag exists to warn about, with the warning disarmed.

*The rule that was derived from it:*

> **`info` travels with a structure that may LEAVE this calculation to start
> another. It does not travel into a structure derived FOR USE INSIDE one.**

By that test: `replace`, `copy`, `concat`, the geometry ops and the sidecar
codec all carry it correctly — they produce structures a person still owns.
`as_structure()` correctly does not — it produces a rung's input.

**The concerns, stated so they are examined rather than inherited:**

1. It was derived from **one** case. The other `info` clusters are hypothetical
   — `calculation` is the only shipped one — so "may leave this calculation"
   has been tested against a single kind of content.
2. It is a rule about INTENT (*may this structure leave?*) and nothing in the
   code can check intent. So it guides an author and cannot be enforced,
   which is a weaker thing than § 2.2a's other rules.
3. It does not say what should happen to the **other** fields
   `as_structure()` drops — `annotations` and the identity columns. Dropping
   `regions` is clearly right (a bulk lead has no partition). The other two
   were not examined.

**What is asked of the next person:** confirm or correct the rule against a
second case, then write it into `model/structure.md` § 2.2a as the test § 2.2a
currently lacks — or, if it does not survive, say what does and fix the
comment in `wizard.as_structure()` that now states it.
