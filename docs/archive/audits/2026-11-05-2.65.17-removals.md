It did not trace **design**: why the item was built, what it protects, and what protects that now. This document is that tracing. The person read it before anything was committed; their decisions are below. **The table at the end is the second reader's, as it stood before those decisions**: its rows on what was asked and restored say *ASK*, and the decision is the section below.

## How each item was judged

1. **The list comes from the code**, by comparing the syntax trees of v2.65.16 and the checkout: every definition, argument and record field gone, plus every test function gone, the page functions, the catalogue row, and every `raise`, `if`, `except` removed from functions that survive (168 such lines read).
2. **A role comes before a verdict:** guard, extension, record, script API, alias or plumbing, judged by what the item says it is for. **A check nobody calls is a guard that is not connected, not dead code.** A field nobody reads may be what is left of a guard someone lost, so its old reader is found in history.
3. **History:** `git log -S` for the commit that added each item and the commit that took its reader away.
4. **Promises:** what the live documents said of it at v2.65.16. All 73 lines of document text removed were read.
5. **A guard is tested by causing its failure** in a scratch copy of the checkout, running the fast tests, and seeing whether anything catches it.
6. **A second reader** went through the same list without being told any verdict: its table is below. Where its verdict rests on a test added during this audit, its row says so.
