{#- ===================== 1. The junction ===================== -#}

1. The junction

Point the calculation at one relaxed junction. Every later stage is derived from it: the sorted copy, both electrode cells, the device, the transmission. Nothing is uploaded or copied here — the junction is cited.

It is one of two things, told apart by what it holds, not its name or location. A relaxation run of molbuilder's own that finished — its folder: its deck and its result, launched through jobset so its run record says how it ended, its region labels in the deck. Or a structure pair that records the optimization its geometry came from — its .xyz, saved from such a run (the Results tab's Export, or molbuilder xv2xyz --from-run), or a frame set a script wrote onto that pair through the structure's own doors — every frame then runs at every voltage of the list. Either answers the basis, the functional and the real-space mesh, and step 2 shows them as the run's, yours to change; a pair brings no pseudopotentials, so step 2 asks for the directory that holds them. A structure that records no optimization is not a citation: relax it first (Structure optimization → Task setup), then cite the run.

Or a transport calculation of molbuilder's own — its folder — to reuse it. A wider question is a new calculation that reuses a finished one: the cards fill from it — its junction, its voltages and their treatment, its values — and you change what the new question asks. Describe writes it into the folder you chose; its prep copies in every group the finished calculation computed as this one would now, and the rest runs here. Its label, its junction and its electrode rename are the finished calculation's.

Once you cite a pair, leave it alone. A new or changed frame set goes in a new file, cited by a new calculation — prep refuses a cited pair that was edited afterwards.

Pick a folder or a pair's .xyz: the line under the button names what it is, or what it is missing, and a frame set's card says what each frame is. The viewer then shows the cited structure with its region labels — use it to check that the electrodes and the bridge are the atoms you meant.

Labels are assigned where the junction is built, on the Molbuilder tab, never here. The convention is L-electrode at low z and R-electrode at high z: atom order follows the geometry, but the bias µ = +V/2 follows the name, so labelling them the other way round biases the other lead. That case is detected below and offers a rename.

Reference — Transport contract: § 3.1 what you can cite, § 4 how region labels drive the five stages.

    Several voltages — the low-bias approximation?

    The bias is the experiment, so it is asked first — and it decides whether the rest matters. At 0.0 the junction is at equilibrium and every field marked non-equilibrium below does nothing. Several values are a sweep, and how each point is computed is the choice above: under the low-bias approximation the device and the transmission run once, at 0 V, and the record computes each voltage's current from T(E, 0); self-consistent, every voltage gets its own device SCF and its own transmission, one job walking the points, each starting from the one before it.

      The five stages, in order

      Each tab in step 4 is one of these stages. What each one computes, what it hands to the next, and which k-points it samples:

      {#- Filled from the rung list the schema route answers -- the same notes each tab opens with (transport/stages.py RUNG_NOTES), each headed by the rung's own number, as its tab is ("1 · seed"). -#}

        k-points: what each stage samples, and why

        A crystal repeats one cell over and over, and so do its electrons' waves: from one copy of the cell to the next, a wave comes back the same apart from a phase shift. A k-point is one such shift. A run cannot follow every shift, so it samples a regular k-point grid of them — for example 4 × 4 × 1 — and averages: more k-points, more accurate, slower.

        Here the electrodes are infinite metal surfaces in the electrode plane (x, y), so the junction's cell repeats sideways, one molecule in each copy. Along the transport direction (z) the junction runs from one lead to the other and does not repeat; a lead on its own is bulk metal, which does.

        Three k-point settings, each set in one place: the shared k-point grid (step 2, k-point grid), the leads' k-points along z (each lead's tab) and the transmission k-point grid (the transmission's tab).

        Seed

        The shared k-point grid in the electrode plane, and one k-point along z. Its cell is long along z, and its density is only where the device starts, so one point there is enough.

        Each lead

        The shared k-point grid in the electrode plane, and it must be: the device uses each lead as the bulk metal of its own electrode region, so both must be sampled on the same k-points — TranSIESTA stops if they are not.

        Many k-points along z — 100, on the lead's tab (warned below 50). A lead is a short cell of bulk metal, endless along z, and a short cell needs many points along its length.

        The device adds the endless lead through the self-energy, a correction that stands in for the lead beyond the cell. It is built from this run's Hamiltonian and aligned to this run's Fermi level, so too few k-points here shift every T(E), and no error says so.

        Device

        The shared k-point grid in the electrode plane, and one k-point along z. Along z the device does not repeat: the leads' self-energies stand in for everything beyond the cell, and TranSIESTA forces one point there.

        Transmission

        Its own transmission k-point grid in the electrode plane, finer than the shared one. T(E) changes sharply from one k-point to the next, where the density is smooth, so it needs more points.

        Left blank it is three times the shared grid, and follows it; set it on the transmission's tab and it is yours. It may differ from the shared grid because tbtrans rebuilds each lead's self-energy at its own k-points. One k-point along z.

        Setting them up, in order

        1. The shared k-point grid starts as the junction you cited. Raise each count about 1.5× and run the leads and the device again: it is settled when the leads' Fermi level and T(E) at the Fermi level stay put.
        2. The leads' k-points along z: keep 100. Raising the count must not move a lead's Fermi level — the Results tab shows both leads'.
        3. The transmission k-point grid: run it at its start, then about 1.5× finer (12 → 18), and lay the two T(E) over each other. It is settled when T(E) at the Fermi level moves by less than about 1 % and no peak moves. Keep that grid for every voltage.

        A finer transmission grid cannot rescue a shared grid that was too coarse: the device's Hamiltonian itself would be wrong, and more k-points do not fix it.

        Example

        A gold junction three gold atoms wide (about 8.7 Å side to side): a 4 × 4 × 1 shared k-point grid, 100 k-points along z on each lead, and a 12 × 12 × 1 transmission k-point grid.

        One k-point in a repeating direction

        A count of 1 along x or y, while the gold surface repeats there, samples the metal's bands at one place only. It is known to add false peaks to T(E) and to shift the conductance by tens of per cent, so Describe warns beside the grid. It is a warning, not a refusal: one point is fine for a quick first test, and the warning is never said for a chain in a box, where nothing repeats sideways.

        Sources

        The SIESTA 5.4.2 manual and source (ts_electrode.F90, ts_kpoint_scf.F90, m_ts_electrode.F90, tbt_init.F90); Brandbyge et al. 2002; Lopez Sancho et al. 1985; Papior et al. 2017; Thygesen and Jacobsen 2005; Strange et al. 2008 — in the Transport contract § 0.2–0.3b, with a diagram of what each stage hands the next.

        SCF settings: each stage has its own

        The seed, both leads and the device each run their own SCF — the loop that repeats until the electrons settle — so each of their tabs carries the same SCF settings with its own values, and a value set on one tab reaches that stage alone. Each starts from the values the cited relaxation converged with, marked from the junction you cited. The transmission runs no SCF: it reads the device's and the leads' finished results, so T(E) is only as settled as those runs.

        Example — if the device's SCF swings back and forth instead of settling, lower the mixing weight on the device's tab; the seed and the leads keep theirs.

        After you describe

        {#- The road after describing — the composite's own quickstart. -#}
        1. Prepare the task, then launch it group by group — jobset prep task prepares every stage at once, from the command line or from Task setup's Prep on that folder; each jobset launch task sends the next group whose upstream has finished: first the seed and both leads as one job; run it again once they have finished for the device, and again for the transmission. Each takes what it needs from the newest finished runs before it when it is first launched.
        2. Why five and not one: tbtrans needs each lead's converged bulk Hamiltonian before it can build that lead's self-energy, and the device needs both. A self-consistent sweep is one run per stage with a point per voltage — one job walks them, each device point starting from the one before it; under the low-bias approximation the device and the transmission run once, at 0 V.
        3. Read it back on the Results tab, or with summarize task, which writes <label>.transport.json: T(E) per point, the conductance at E_F, and the I–V — each point's TBtrans current, or the record's own from T(E, 0) under the approximation.
