1. The junction

Point the calculation at one directory that already holds a relaxed junction. Every later stage is derived from it: the sorted copy, both electrode cells, the device, the transmission. Nothing is uploaded or copied here — the directory is cited.

A directory qualifies by its files, not its name or location: it is a relaxation run of molbuilder's own that finished — its one .fdf and one .XV, launched through jobset so its run record says how it ended, its region labels in the deck or the .molstruct.json beside it. Its deck answers the basis, functional and mesh, and step 2 shows them as the run's, yours to change. A structure saved from the Molbuilder tab is not a citation: relax it first (Structure optimization → Task setup), then cite the run.

Pick any directory: the line under the button names what it is, or what it is missing. The viewer then shows the cited structure with its region labels — use it to check that the electrodes and the bridge are the atoms you meant.

Labels are assigned where the junction is built, on the Molbuilder tab, never here. The convention is L-electrode at low z and R-electrode at high z: atom order follows the geometry, but the bias µ = +V/2 follows the name, so labelling them the other way round biases the other lead. That case is detected below and offers a rename.

    Several voltages — the low-bias approximation?

    The bias is the experiment, so it is asked first — and it decides whether the rest matters. At 0.0 the junction is at equilibrium and every field marked non-equilibrium below does nothing. Several values are a sweep, and how each point is computed is the choice above: under the low-bias approximation the device and the transmission run once, at 0 V, and the record computes each voltage's current from T(E, 0); self-consistent, every voltage gets its own device SCF and its own transmission, one job walking the points, each starting from the one before it.

    One tab per rung, in the order they run. Pick a rung; its tab opens with what that rung computes and hands on, then its own settings first and the shared SCF, output and runtime cards folded beneath. A value typed on a tab is that rung's alone. Basis, functional, mesh and the rest of step 2 are not here: they bind every rung, and a per-rung value for one is refused by name. The grey tag under a field is the fdf keyword it writes; help expands the reasoning.

      {#- The road after describing — the composite's own quickstart. -#}
      What happens after this — the five stages, in order
      1. Prepare and launch the task from Task setup on that folder, or with jobset prep task / launch task. Each prep offers the stages that are ready: first the seed and both leads, as one job; then the device, once they have finished; then the transmission. Each gathers what it needs from the newest finished runs before it.
      2. Why five and not one: tbtrans needs each lead's converged bulk Hamiltonian before it can build that lead's self-energy, and the device needs both. A self-consistent sweep is one run per stage with a point per voltage — one job walks them, each device point starting from the one before it; under the low-bias approximation the device and the transmission run once, at 0 V.
      3. Read it back on the Results tab, or with summarize task, which writes <label>.transport.json: T(E) per point, the conductance at E_F, and the I–V — each point's TBtrans current, or the record's own from T(E, 0) under the approximation.
