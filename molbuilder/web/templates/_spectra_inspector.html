Mode visualisation

{#- HOW BIG the motion is, which is two different questions. DISPLAY exaggerates it so the eye can follow: real vibrational amplitudes are small, and every tool that draws them -- Jmol, Avogadro, Gaussian -- exaggerates the same way. The slider is the size, in angstrom, of the largest-moving atom's swing. PHYSICAL draws how far the atoms actually move. There is no slider for it because there is nothing to choose: the size follows from the mode's own frequency. Zero-point is the T = 0 quantum floor; thermal adds the population of higher states at a temperature you give, and reduces to zero-point as that goes to zero. The two are not interchangeable and the export records which was used, because they do not even share a unit (docs/web/vibrationview.md 12.2). -#}
{#- HOW BIG what you are watching is, in a sentence, directly under the picture it describes. It sat up in the header beside the title, where a squeezed flex row left room for a fragment like "max ±0.173 Å" -- a quantity with nothing to say what it measured. Given its own line it can name the atom, the distance from rest, the bond it is a fraction of, and whether that is real or exaggerated, which is what the number is FOR. Written by _reportSwing in lib/spectra/core.js; the science is docs/web/spectra.md § 4.1. -#}

{#- Getting the animation out. The three formats are not interchangeable: GIF goes anywhere and cannot hold either a smooth gradient or a soft transparent edge; WebM is a video; the PNG sequence is lossless at any size and is what an encoder wants as input, which is why it carries a manifest saying what it is and the ffmpeg line that turns it into whatever a journal asked for. -#}
What do the amplitude choices mean?

The mode's shape is the same in all three — what changes is how big it is drawn.

exaggerated — a drawing choice. The biggest-moving atom swings by the Size slider. Real amplitudes are too small to read, so every tool exaggerates.

zero-point — how far the atoms really move at absolute zero; a quantum oscillator is never still. Falls as 1/√ν̃ and as 1/√mass, so stiff bonds and heavy atoms move less: hydrogen swings 0.41 Å in a 100 cm⁻¹ torsion but only 0.075 Å in a C–H stretch, where carbon manages 0.022 Å.

thermal — adds the higher vibrational levels populated at your temperature. Room temperature is worth only ~207 cm⁻¹, so stiffer modes are frozen out and look identical: ×2.1 at 100 cm⁻¹, ×1.27 at 300, ×1.01 at 1000. Worth setting for torsions and librations, invisible on a stretch.

The full derivation, the constants and why the two normalisations must never be crossed: spectra.md § 4.1.
